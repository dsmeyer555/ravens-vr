Stick and puck

Round 6 of the stick and puck test. Shots now come off the stick the way a real wrist shot does: the puck rides the blade through your sweep and leaves at the angle of the face and the lift of your follow-through. Soft pushes stay close to you, all twenty curves are traced from the shop pictures you sent, and the stick and blade are rebuilt. Press Enter VR and spend about ten minutes stickhandling, pushing the puck ahead while you skate, and shooting.

In the headset: controls
  1. Both hands hold the stick. Your top hand (left, for a right shot) is on the butt end and your lower hand slides along the shaft. Your gloves stay on the shaft: the lower one slides up and down as your hand does, and moving your hand sideways steers the stick, like a real two-hand grip.
  2. Holding the stick. Your top hand always has the stick. Hold the trigger (or grip) on your lower hand to put that hand on the stick too; let go and you are one-handed, for a reach or a poke check. (The old A-button switch is still in the menu, Stick tab.)
  3. The blade stays on the ice. With your hands low the blade rests flat on the ice, and if your hands sit a little high the stick stretches a few centimetres so the blade still touches. Lift your hands on purpose (a saucer pass, a shot, a lift) and it comes off the ice at once. Blade to ice in the menu sets how much help you get.
  4. Shoot like you would on the ice. With the puck on your blade, sweep the stick forward through it: the puck stays on the blade for the whole sweep and leaves as the sweep peaks. It goes up if you open the blade (turn your lower wrist so the face points up) and follow through high; keep the face closed and finish low and it stays low. A hard, fast sweep goes further and higher than a slow one. Shot lift in the menu sets how much the face angle counts: Real only is the plain angle.
  5. Pushing the puck ahead. A gentle push forward sends the puck a metre or two ahead and it stops running away from you, so you can skate onto it.
  6. Arcade play style (the default; Game tab in the menu): once you have the puck it stays on your blade. Sweep the blade the other way and it comes over to the other face, so forehand to backhand and back just works. A real push, pass or shot sends it, and lifting the blade lets it go. Also: your blade near a carrier's puck is a poke check, a ring under every player (gold Ravens, red Visitors, bright on whoever has the puck, flashing white when he is about to poke), a glow under the puck, and a short blink when the game moves you. Simulation turns all that off and gives you the full puck physics.
  7. Puck help (on Normal) catches a pass on your blade, carries the puck while you skate, carries it round with you on a snap turn, and slows a puck you have just nudged so you can catch up with it. It lets go the moment you swing at the puck, so passes and shots are not changed. You feel a soft buzz while the puck is on your blade, a tap for each touch, and a stronger kick for passes and shots.
  8. Get into your ready stance with the blade just above the ice and keep it there for a second: the stick calibrates itself so the blade sits flat. Press Y any time to do it again (it also resets your height).
  9. Left thumbstick skates. Push a little for a slow stride, fully for top speed. In an arc you crossover automatically, and pushing the stick well to the side at speed makes a punch turn (quick, and it costs speed). Left trigger brakes.
  10. Calls in drills and the scrimmage: left stick click shouts "Shoot!" to a teammate with the puck in their end, right stick click shouts "Over! D to D!" to a D with it (in the breakout drill your call decides the side). When a game or drill ends you get a career code (RV-XXXX-XXXX) on the board and in your report: type it into the career game under VR sessions and your teammates there will know how it went.
  11. Talk to your teammates (needs your server, set up on this page under Talk to your teammates): just talk, or hold A if you chose that. Quick calls work out loud: "Shoot!", "Pass!", "D to D!", "Same side!". Anything longer goes to the teammate you are looking at (or the nearest one), who answers out loud; at a stoppage or between reps is best. Finished games and drills go to your career game by themselves.
  12. Call for the puck in drills and the scrimmage: squeeze the grip on your top hand, or tap your blade on the ice twice. An open teammate taps his stick when he wants it from you.
  13. Right thumbstick left and right turns you. Up and down sets the stick length (it starts at 58 in). Click it to change curve.
  14. B puts the puck back in front of you. X (left) opens the menu (top speed is in there now): point at it with either controller and pull the trigger. The right thumbstick left and right changes tab. The game stands still while it is open.
  15. The scoreboard over centre ice shows your speed, your last hit and best, goals, the frame rate, your stick length, whether you are keeping the puck, and the wrist roll the game sees. If you feel unwell, take the headset off.
  16. When you leave VR, the Last VR session card appears at the bottom of this page. It is saved if you reload.
